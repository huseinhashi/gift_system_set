CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION 
1.1 Background of the Study 
The retail industry has undergone a profound transformation in recent years, driven by 
the rapid adoption of digital technologies and the global expansion of e-commerce. As 
highlighted by (Reinartz et al., 2019), the shift from traditional brick-and-mortar retail to 
digital platforms has redefined customer interactions, operational efficiency, and value 
creation across the retail value chain. This digital transformation enables businesses to 
streamline processes such as inventory management, customer relationship management 
(CRM), and logistics, while also fostering personalized and immersive shopping 
experiences. For small and medium enterprises (SMEs), particularly in underserved 
communities, embracing these technologies is critical to remaining competitive and 
expanding market reach. 
In regions like Somalia, where economic infrastructure is evolving, flower and gift shops 
face unique challenges. Many businesses in the Somali community still rely on manual 
processes for order management, inventory tracking, and customer engagement, leading 
to inefficiencies and limited scalability. Additionally, the lack of integrated digital 
platforms restricts access to broader markets and hampers customer satisfaction. The 
absence of secure, localized online payment systems further exacerbates these challenges, 
as highlighted by (Houcheimi & Mezei, 2024). Their research underscores how secure 
payment solutions, such as mobile money platforms, are pivotal for financial inclusion 
and e-tailing growth in underbanked regions. Without reliable payment gateways tailored 
to local preferences—like Somalia’s widespread use of Waafi—businesses struggle to 
build trust and facilitate seamless transactions. 
Logistical inefficiencies, particularly in last-mile delivery, present another critical barrier. 
(Mishra, 2024) emphasize that optimizing last-mile logistics is essential for e-commerce 
success, especially in urban areas with infrastructural constraints. For Somali flower and 
1 
gift shops, unreliable delivery systems and fragmented coordination between suppliers, 
retailers, and customers often result in delayed orders and increased operational costs. 
These challenges are compounded by limited access to real-time inventory tracking and 
analytics, which are vital for informed decision-making and resource allocation. 
Against this backdrop, there is an urgent need for a tailored digital solution that addresses 
the specific needs of Somali flower and gift shops. A dynamic web portal integrated with 
a mobile application can bridge these gaps by automating core operations—such as order 
processing, CRM, and inventory management—while incorporating localized payment 
systems and logistics optimization. By leveraging insights from digital transformation 
frameworks (Reinartz et al., 2019),secure transaction model (Houcheimi & Mezei, 2024), 
and last-mile delivery strategies, (Mishra, 2024), this system aims to empower Somali 
SMEs with tools to enhance operational efficiency, customer satisfaction, and market 
adaptability in an increasingly digital economy. 
1.2 Problem Statement 
The flower and gift shop industry in Somalia operates with significant inefficiencies due 
to outdated, manual processes and a lack of integrated digital tools tailored to local needs. 
Key challenges include: 
1. Manual Operations: 
o Inventory management relies on paper-based tracking, leading to stock 
discrepancies (e.g., overstocking perishable flowers or stockouts during 
peak demand). 
o Order processing and billing are time-consuming, with no real-time 
updates for customers or staff. 
2. Fragmented Customer Experience: 
2 
o Customers lack a centralized platform to browse products, place orders, 
track deliveries, or engage with promotions, reducing satisfaction and 
loyalty. 
3. Limited Local Payment Integration: 
o Most shops cannot accept popular mobile money platforms like Waafi, 
forcing reliance on cash transactions that increase fraud risks and limit 
scalability. 
4. Inefficient Delivery Logistics: 
o Poor coordination with logistics providers causes delays, particularly for 
perishable goods requiring temperature-controlled delivery. 
5. Lack of Data-Driven Insights: 
o Owners lack tools to analyze sales trends, track employee performance, or 
optimize promotions, hindering decision-making. 
These issues underscore the need for a unified platform that integrates essential business 
functions while addressing Somalia’s unique logistical, cultural, and financial 
requirements.

2.3 Online Payment Systems and Their Importance in E-Commerce 
In the digital age, online payment systems play a pivotal role in fostering e-commerce 
growth by ensuring secure transactions, reducing fraud risks, and increasing customer 
trust. As businesses continue to transition to digital platforms, the adoption of robust 
payment gateways has become essential for sustainable economic activities. 
One of the fundamental aspects of online payment security is multi-factor authentication 
(MFA), which enhances transaction security by incorporating biometric verification, 
encryption, and location-based authentication (Alamleh et al., 2023), emphasize that 
modern mobile payment systems should leverage these authentication methods to 
mitigate fraud risks and ensure legitimacy in financial transactions. Near Field 
Communication (NFC) payments, for example, have emerged as a secure and efficient 
11 
alternative, allowing consumers to complete transactions swiftly while benefiting from 
advanced encryption techniques (Alamleh et al., 2023). 
The security of electronic payment systems is further reinforced by tokenization, which 
replaces sensitive credit card information with unique identifiers. According to (Solat, 
2017),tokenization enhances payment security by minimizing exposure to financial data, 
thereby reducing the risk of cyber fraud. Additionally, 3D Secure authentication 
protocols, such as Verified-by-Visa and MasterCard SecureCode, add another layer of 
protection by requiring customers to verify their identities before completing online 
purchases (Solat, 2017). 
For emerging markets, localized payment solutions are critical to fostering adoption and 
financial inclusion. The recent collaboration between Mastercard and Premier Bank in 
Somalia demonstrates how tailored digital payment gateways can expand e-commerce 
opportunities. By enabling Somali businesses to accept international payments securely, 
this initiative has not only increased market reach but also contributed to economic 
growth (Mastercard, 2024). The integration of digital payments aligns with global 
financial inclusion goals and supports local businesses in navigating the evolving e
commerce landscape. 
Ultimately, online payment systems serve as the backbone of digital commerce, offering 
security, efficiency, and inclusivity. As technological advancements continue to shape 
the financial sector, businesses must prioritize secure payment infrastructures to enhance 
customer confidence and drive e-commerce success. 
2.4 Logistics and Delivery Optimization in E-Commerce 
Summar Logistics and delivery optimization are critical components of e-commerce, 
particularly for businesses dealing with perishable goods like flowers, where timely 
delivery directly impacts customer satisfaction and product quality. This section explores 
12 
the challenges and strategies for optimizing last-mile delivery, focusing on route 
optimization, real-time tracking, and delivery management, all of which are essential for 
a seamless online shopping experience. 
2.4.1 Last-Mile Delivery Challenges 
The last mile of delivery—the final leg from distribution centers to customers—is often 
the most complex and costly phase of the supply chain. Urban logistics face unique 
challenges, including traffic congestion, high population density, and limited 
infrastructure. According to (Mishra, 2024), last-mile delivery can account for up to 53% 
of total shipping costs due to factors such as fuel prices, vehicle maintenance, and labor 
expenses. Additionally, consumer expectations for faster and more flexible delivery 
options, such as same-day or one-hour delivery, further complicate logistics operations. 
These challenges are exacerbated in regions with underdeveloped infrastructure, such as 
Somalia, where informal addressing systems and fragmented transportation networks add 
layers of complexity. 
2.4.2 Strategies for Optimization 
To address these challenges, several strategies have been proposed and implemented in 
urban logistics. Route optimization algorithms, powered by artificial intelligence (AI) and 
machine learning (ML), analyze real-time traffic data, delivery priorities, and road 
conditions to determine the most efficient delivery routes. These tools reduce delivery 
times and fuel consumption, improving overall efficiency (Mishra, 2024). Crowd-sourced 
delivery models, which leverage independent contractors or gig workers, offer flexibility 
and scalability, particularly during peak demand periods. Platforms like Uber Eats and 
Amazon Flex demonstrate the potential of this approach to enhance delivery capabilities 
without significant investment in fleet expansion. 
13 
Micro-hubs, or intermediate storage locations, are another effective strategy. These hubs 
allow for the consolidation of orders, reducing the need for large delivery vehicles in 
dense urban areas. By enabling the use of eco-friendly transport modes, such as bicycles 
or electric scooters, for the final leg of delivery, micro-hubs contribute to sustainability 
while lowering operational costs. Electric vehicles (EVs) and autonomous delivery 
systems, including drones, are also gaining traction as sustainable alternatives to 
traditional delivery vans, reducing carbon footprints and addressing environmental 
concerns (Mishra, 2024). 
2.4.3 Technology-Driven Solutions 
The integration of advanced technologies is transforming logistics operations. (Llivisaca 
et al., 2022)highlights the role of AI-powered demand forecasting in predicting seasonal 
spikes and optimizing inventory placement. IoT sensors monitor storage conditions, such 
as temperature for perishable goods, and trigger automated replenishment alerts, ensuring 
product freshness. Blockchain technology enhances transparency and traceability, 
particularly for ethically sourced products, while omnichannel strategies unify inventory 
and delivery processes across multiple platforms. 
Data-driven decision-making is another critical component of logistics optimization. By 
leveraging big data analytics, businesses can identify patterns in consumer behavior, peak 
delivery times, and urban traffic trends. This information enables proactive decision
making, such as adjusting delivery resources to meet demand surges, ensuring timely and 
cost-effective deliveries (Llivisaca et al., 2022). 
2.4.4 Customer-Centric Logistics 
Customer satisfaction is closely tied to the efficiency of logistics operations. (Ahmed, 
2024)emphasizes the importance of lead time management, inventory control, and 
transportation efficiency in enhancing the customer experience. The study found that lead 
14 
time management has the strongest impact on customer satisfaction (coefficient = 0.63), 
followed by inventory management (0.211) and transportation (0.134). Real-time 
tracking and flexible delivery options, such as scheduled pickups or same-day delivery, 
are essential for meeting customer expectations and reducing failed delivery attempts. 
2.4.5 Sustainability and Cost Efficiency 
Sustainability is a growing concern in logistics, particularly in urban areas where delivery 
vehicles contribute to air pollution and traffic congestion. Transitioning to electric 
vehicles, optimizing delivery routes to minimize fuel consumption, and implementing 
micro-hubs are effective strategies for reducing environmental impacts while lowering 
operational costs. Cross-docking, a logistics strategy that minimizes warehousing by 
directly transferring goods from incoming to outgoing vehicles, also reduces inventory 
holding costs and improves delivery efficiency (Llivisaca et al., 2022) 
By adopting advanced technologies, such as AI-driven route optimizations and enabled 
inventory management, businesses can streamline operations and enhance customer 
satisfaction. These approaches align with the broader goals of creating efficient, scalable, 
and customer-centric logistics systems, ensuring a seamless shopping experience for 
consumers.

2.5.1 The Role of Digitalization in Inventory Management 
The logistics performance of retail businesses, particularly in the fast-moving consumer 
goods (FMCG) sector, is significantly influenced by effective inventory management. 
15 
(Chauhan et al., 2023) emphasize that digitalization plays a crucial role in optimizing 
transportation and warehousing performance, which directly impacts logistics 
distribution performance. By adopting digital tools such as warehouse management 
systems (WMS) and transportation management systems (TMS), businesses can enhance 
operational efficiency, reduce costs, and improve customer satisfaction. The integration 
of these technologies allows for real-time inventory tracking, which is essential for 
managing perishable goods like flowers. 
2.5.2 Predictive Analytics and AI in Stock Control 
AI-powered predictive analytics are becoming increasingly important in inventory 
management. (Li & Shi, 2024) discuss the optimization of logistics networks, particularly 
in the context of prepositioned warehouses, where maintaining product freshness is 
critical. Their research highlights the use of mathematical optimization models to manage 
inventory effectively, ensuring that stock levels are aligned with consumer demand. By 
leveraging AI algorithms, businesses can analyze historical sales data and seasonal trends 
to forecast demand accurately, thereby minimizing the risks of stockouts and 
overstocking. 
2.5.3 Enhancing Efficiency through Technology 
The implementation of advanced technologies in inventory management is essential for 
improving efficiency.(Yang et al., 2023) propose a deep reinforcement learning-based 
logistics management model (DELLMM) that optimizes logistics distribution. Their 
findings indicate that integrating AI and machine learning into inventory management 
processes can lead to significant improvements in operational efficiency. By utilizing 
real-time data and self-learning algorithms, businesses can enhance their inventory 
control strategies, ensuring timely replenishment and reducing waste. 
16 
2.5.4 Challenges in Inventory Management 
Despite the advancements in inventory management technologies, challenges remain, 
particularly in the context of perishable goods. The need for timely deliveries and 
maintaining product quality adds complexity to inventory management. (Chauhan et al., 
2023) note that inefficiencies in transportation and warehousing can lead to increased 
costs and reduced profitability for retailers. Therefore, addressing these challenges 
through digitalization and optimization strategies is crucial for enhancing inventory 
management in the retail sector. 
2.5.5 Future Directions in Inventory Management 
As the retail landscape continues to evolve, the integration of AI and digital technologies 
in inventory management will be vital for success. The research by (Li & Shi, 2024) 
suggests that future studies should focus on the development of more sophisticated 
inventory management models that account for the unique characteristics of perishable 
goods. By embracing innovative technologies and optimizing logistics networks, retailers 
can improve their inventory management practices, ultimately leading to enhanced 
customer satisfaction and business growth. 
The integration of AI-powered stock tracking and predictive analytics in inventory 
management is essential for improving the efficiency and accuracy of retail systems. The 
findings from the relevant research underscore the importance of adopting digital 
technologies to optimize inventory control processes, particularly for businesses dealing 
with perishable goods like flowers. By leveraging these advancements, retailers can 
navigate the complexities of modern supply chains and ensure timely delivery of 
products, contributing to their overall success in a competitive marketplace.

2.6.1 The Role of CRM Systems in Customer Engagement 
Customer Relationship Management (CRM) systems are essential for managing

This personalization not only enhances customer satisfaction but also fosters loyalty, as 
customers feel valued and understood. 
CRM systems also facilitate seamless communication across multiple channels, ensuring

of engagement strengthens the customer-business relationship and encourages repeat 
purchases. 
2.6.2 Personalized Marketing and Data Analytics 
Personalized marketing is a key driver of customer engagement in online retail. 
(Houcheimi & Mezei, 2024) highlight that secure online payment systems, combined 
with data analytics, enable businesses to offer personalized recommendations and 
promotions. For example, a customer who frequently purchases roses may receive 
targeted offers for similar products or complementary items like vases or chocolates. This 
18 
approach not only increases sales but also enhances the customer experience by making 
shopping more convenient and relevant. 
Data analytics plays a crucial role in understanding customer behavior and predicting 
future trends. By analyzing purchase patterns, businesses can identify which products are 
most popular during specific seasons or events, allowing them to optimize inventory and 
marketing strategies. For flower and gift shops, this means being able to anticipate

2.6.3 Enhancing Customer Loyalty through Engagement 
Customer loyalty is a critical factor for the long-term success of any retail business. 
(Reinartz et al., 2019) emphasize that digital tools, such as loyalty programs and 
personalized offers, are effective in retaining customers. For instance, a flower shop could 
implement a points-based loyalty program where customers earn rewards for every 
purchase. These rewards can then be redeemed for discounts or free products, 
incentivizing repeat business. 
Moreover, engaging customers through social media and email marketing can further 
strengthen loyalty. By sharing content such as flower care tips, gift ideas, or behind-the
scenes glimpses of the business, flower and gift shops can create a sense of community 
and connection with their customers. This not only keeps the brand top-of-mind but also 
encourages customers to return for future purchases.

shopping experience. For example, an AI-driven chatbot can assist customers in selecting

is out for delivery, reducing uncertainty and improving satisfaction.

shops, as customers may browse products on their mobile devices but complete the 
purchase on a desktop or vice versa.

content to fit the screen size of the device being used. This ensures that customers can 
easily navigate the platform, view product details, and make purchases regardless of the 
device they are using.
